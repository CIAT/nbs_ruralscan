## 2026-10-19 · mangrove_restoration · welfare outcomes came from the bundle, not the trees

The IEG PPAR of the Vietnam Coastal Wetlands project reports poverty −38% and incomes +55% in
the project area — and attributes them to resettlement and technology transfer, **not** to the
mangrove planting. Pete 2026-10-19: "it is not the mangrove, we cannot count it — it is other
innovation bundled with the restoration that were important." No EV unit credits these numbers
to the practice (the extraction lane was briefed to withhold them).

**Feasibility implication:** a mangrove (or any restoration) investment that promises household
welfare outcomes must budget the complementary livelihood package explicitly — resettlement
support, technology transfer, alternative incomes. The trees alone did not deliver the welfare
result in the one ex-post WB evaluation we hold. Source: `wb_ieg_ppar_vietnam_coastal_wetlands_2009`.

## 2026-10-19 · wetland_management (creation) · sediment trapping decays without maintenance

Created riverine basins in Ohio showed sediment and total-phosphorus retention diminishing
after 10 years of river flow, while nitrate and soluble-reactive-phosphorus removal persisted
(`ev_sediment_retention_mits05_5`). Pete 2026-10-19: the wetland traps already-eroded sediment;
it does not prevent the landscape erosion that generates it (the erosion-risk route is removed;
the unit votes on water quality only).

**Feasibility implication:** sediment management / dredging belongs in the recurrent-cost case
of any created wetland whose purpose includes trapping; the particulate service ages out as the
basin fills. Pair the wetland with upstream erosion control if landscape soil loss is the
problem being bought.

## 2026-10-16 · wetland_management · existing-use income is the opportunity cost of rewetting

Households already draw income from the wetlands restoration would alter: cultivation in
Rwanda, product sales in Uganda, fish catches in Lao PDR. Pete's ruling makes these non-voting
notes in the poverty and production cells (they are the counterfactual, not a benefit of
restoring). Record: `methodology/decisions/2026-10-15_wetland_class_defects.md`
(existing_wetland_home_family_vote).

**Feasibility implication:** a rewetting or restoration design must count forgone wetland-use
income as a project cost and plan replacement livelihoods — the GCF Uganda project did this by
design (alternative-livelihood packages alongside demarcation and cultivation restrictions).

## 2026-10-15 · wetland_management (coastal) · budget for steady edge retreat, not storm repair

Most salt-marsh deterioration is caused by moderate storms of monthly frequency; intense
hurricanes contribute less than 1% to long-term marsh erosion (`ev_erosion_hazard_leon17_5/6`).
The maintenance burden is continuous, not event-driven.

**Feasibility implication:** coastal wetland investments should budget ongoing edge maintenance
under the everyday wave climate rather than a post-cyclone repair reserve.
