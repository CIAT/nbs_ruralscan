## One correction to my own build

I had the gas rule firing on any single gas, which would have made the forest-restoration and
agroforestry carbon cells data deficient over a methane question nobody asked, since their evidence is
carbon dioxide removal with no gas trade-off. Your wording is narrower than my implementation was, so
the rule now fires only where methane or nitrous oxide is actually in the pool.

## Two things worth your eye

**1. Forest restoration, water stress, reads "strongly reduces water stress" and probably should not.**
Agreement is barely above a coin flip. The positive side is mostly practitioner ratings plus one
modelled yield projection; the negative side is the best-designed evidence in the row, two
meta-analyses finding catchment water yield and baseflow down in most cases, with the largest
proportional losses at the driest sites. That is the opposite of what the class says, and it is worst
in exactly the dry contexts this layer is meant to serve. It looks like one cell doing two jobs:
local infiltration and dry-season supply on one side, catchment yield on the other.

**2. Five units carry a sign that looks wrong on its own quote**, independent of your rulings. The
clearest: a silvopastoral system recorded as a carbon gain while its quote shows the system losing
19.65 per cent of soil carbon, positive only against a slash-and-burn arm the claim never names.
These are going to review rather than being edited quietly.

## Still missing as ontology

Peat subsidence rate, wave attenuation, establishment survival, greenhouse gas as a variable in its
own right, and a home for distributional equity. The first is the primary measured outcome of our best
tropical evidence, so it contributes nothing today. None of these was invented to fill a gap.
