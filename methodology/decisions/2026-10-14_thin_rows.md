# Should a table row exist when the evidence behind it is one sentence?

**For:** Pete. **From:** the orchestrator, 2026-10-14. **Needs:** one answer, option A, B or C.

## The question in one line

The engine currently publishes a scorecard row whenever a cell has any evidence at all, even a
single second-hand sentence, and marks it very low confidence. Should those rows be published
at all, or should the table say nothing there?

## Why it is being asked now

The riparian and wetland round filled both tables for the first time. Most rows are built from
real pooled evidence. A handful are not. The prose writers flagged them rather than dressing
them up, which is how we found them.

Four examples, all real rows in the tables today:

| row | what is behind it |
|---|---|
| wetland, erosion | one unit, and it measures sediment building up *inside* a created wetland, not erosion on farmland |
| wetland peatland, flood | one sentence in a book chapter describing what *degradation* did, with no restoration measurement |
| wetland rewetting, carbon | two expert assertions from one review, no number in either, and no mention of methane |
| riparian, flood | four units, every one of them second-hand, none a measurement |

Each of these already carries the engine's lowest confidence levels, and the prose states the
weakness plainly. So nothing here is hidden. The question is whether "shown but marked weak" is
the right answer for a tool whose output a Task Team Leader will read quickly.

## What is at stake

A scorecard that shows a class for every cell looks complete. A reader scanning it sees
"wetland restoration reduces flood impact" and moves on; the confidence column is smaller than
the class column and reads as a footnote. The risk is a number being taken seriously that no one
measured.

The opposite risk is just as real. If we suppress thin rows, the table shows blanks, and a blank
is easy to read as "this does not work" when it actually means "nobody has looked". Our own
riparian flood row is a good example: buffers plausibly do something for flooding, we simply
have no measurement of it in hand.

## The options

**A. Publish everything, as now.** Every cell with any evidence gets a class, with confidence
and the caveats carrying the warning. Nothing changes.

**B. Floor the evidence, and say why the cell is empty.** A row is published only if it clears a
minimum: at least two independent sources, or at least one measured (not asserted, not
second-hand) finding. Below that the cell reports "not enough evidence yet" instead of a class,
and names what is missing. The evidence stays in the register and the row reappears the moment
a second source lands.

**C. Publish, but separate the measured from the asserted.** Keep every row, and add a visible
marker to the class itself when nothing behind it was measured, so "reduces flood impact,
asserted only" is distinguishable from "reduces flood impact, measured" at a glance.

## My recommendation

**B**, with the empty cell required to say what is missing rather than just going blank.

The reason is the scope line we already hold elsewhere. This is a scoping tool: it tells a Task
Team Leader where to look and what to check next. "No evidence yet, two sources needed" is
genuinely useful at that stage. A class derived from one second-hand sentence is not — it is a
guess wearing the same clothes as a result, and it will be read as one.

B also costs little. By my count it would withdraw four wetland rows and one riparian row today,
all of which we already know we cannot defend, and the engine's existing two-source rule for
family rows means the machinery is already there.

If you would rather not lose coverage this early, **C** is a reasonable halfway house, and it is
the better choice if you expect the pilot to be judged on how complete the tables look.
