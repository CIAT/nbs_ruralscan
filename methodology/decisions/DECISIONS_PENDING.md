## Decision 1 — forest restoration says it strongly reduces water stress. The best evidence says the opposite.

**The row:** the forest-restoration scorecard's water-stress entry reads *strongly reduces*.
**The problem:** agreement is barely above a coin flip. The positive side is mostly practitioner
ratings and one modelled projection. The negative side is the best-designed evidence in the row:
two meta-analyses finding catchment water yield and baseflow DOWN in most cases, with the largest
losses at the driest sites — exactly the contexts this tool serves.
**Why it happens:** one cell is answering two different questions. Trees improve infiltration and
local soil moisture; they also transpire, cutting catchment yield. Both bodies of evidence are
right, about different things.

- **A. Split the cell** — local infiltration and dry-season supply as one target, catchment water
  yield as another. *(my recommendation)*
- **B. Re-derive the class with study design dominating** — the meta-analyses would flip it negative.
- **C. Leave it.**

**Answer with:** "water stress: A" (or B / C).

## Decision 2 — should income from EXISTING wetlands vote on what restoring one achieves?

**The row:** the rewetting family's poverty entry reads *moderately positive*.
**The problem:** its positive votes are income households already draw from wetlands that were
drained for farming decades ago (Rwandan rice, Ugandan wetland products, Lao fish catches). The
measured restoration studies in the same row — both Poyang studies and the Uganda impact
evaluation — are negative or null. Existing-use income is the OPPORTUNITY COST of rewetting;
letting it vote as a benefit inverts the row.
**Current rule:** your ratified comparator policy keeps existing-wetland evidence out of the
roll-up but lets it vote in its "home" family row. This asks whether that home-row vote is right
for livelihood cells.

- **A. Existing-use units become a non-voting note in the home family too**, exactly as the
  reference-shortfall units you already ruled on. *(my recommendation, at least for poverty and
  production cells)*
- **B. Keep them voting in the home family** (the current ratified behaviour).
- **C. Give existing-wetland use its own labelled row**, separate from restoration.

**Answer with:** "existing wetland: A" (or B / C).
