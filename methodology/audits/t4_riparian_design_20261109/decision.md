# Riparian buffer widths and planning geometry

Pete approved keeping six source profiles for design and planning on 9 November 2026. Both the planted and naturally restored buffer recipes retain the profiles with numerical calibration held. No shared distance-to-water score, universal buffer width, eligibility mask or MCDA weight was approved. These are linear buffer applications. A strip width, a sampling window and a pixel’s distance to water represent different quantities.

| Source and locator | Retained use |
|---|---|
| Mello et al. (2018), PDF p. 2 | Brazilian land-use and water-quality analysis delineated 30 m around rivers and 50 m around springs. The partial application of the cited law omitted reduced-width cases. Retain study geometry and regulatory context. Legal screening in a new jurisdiction requires its applicable law. |
| Dala-Corte et al. (2020), PDF p. 9 | Retain the biodiversity recommendation of 50 m of native reserve on each bank of small Brazilian streams, 100 m across both banks. Preserve authors’ warning about precision, their fixed analysis buffers, and potential benefits of wider reserves. |
| Zhao et al. (2013), PDF p. 4 | The authors selected a 210 m analysis window (seven pixels) for restoration prioritisation. Keep this spatial-planning method and distinguish the analysis extent from the service studies it cites. |
| Cole et al. (2020), PDF p. 3 | Retain the review’s account of Schmitt et al. (1999), including 7.5 m strips for sediment and sediment-bound pollutants, and 15 m for soluble pollutants. Retain pollutant, soil, vegetation and slope dependence. |
| Schultz et al. (1995), PDF p. 23 | Retain Forest Service/NRCS guidance on successive near-stream woody, managed woody and grass/forb zones (about 4.6 m, at least 18.3 m and at least 6.1 m). Keep these zone widths distinct from the authors’ own 20 m Iowa demonstration. |
| Tiwari et al. (2016), PDF p. 5 | Keep the approximate groundwater-discharge hotspot extending 30 m from the perennial stream and 10 m along the intermittent stream, used for an economic comparison of retained forest buffers. The existing planted-family card records its provenance; the study provides no planted-buffer establishment response. |

A site 10 m from a stream can fall inside Dala-Corte’s recommended reserve. Interpreting 50 m as the minimum eligible distance reverses the conservation recommendation. Zhao’s chosen analysis window gives no information about establishment at 211 m. Neither example supplies a score on either side of a boundary. The six values remain in source units and are never averaged. Three publications per family document different uses; this count supplies no numerical support weight.

The accepted recipe before this decision contained zero mapping rows. An earlier draft combined widths and windows into a 50–210 m range for the naturally restored family and a 30 m ceiling for the planted family. The two approved holds preserve the historical references and block replay of those drafts as scoring rules. The distance layer can still support a separately specified planning geometry. A future scored relation requires practice-specific evidence for the response of establishment to site distance, a matching spatial definition, and a new review.
