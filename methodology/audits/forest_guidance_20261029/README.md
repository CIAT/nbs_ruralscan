## What the evidence retains

Mahdavi uses pH, soil texture, slope, aspect and elevation for each of Quercus persica, Pistacia atlantica and Amygdalus scoparia. Fifteen records preserve that **variable use**, with `model_use.used=true`, `claim_scope=species_specific`, a taxon, native quotes and separately anchored method rationale. They do not imply fifteen independent studies. The source's Amygdalous/Amygdalus spelling variation is documented without asserting a taxonomic synonym.

All fifteen suitability directions remain `unclear`: the maps show spatial suitable/unsuitable classes, but do not print a variable-to-response crosswalk. No rule was reconstructed by overlaying small raster illustrations. Soil-texture labels are retained as printed; a USDA crosswalk is unresolved. EC lacks a canonical match, organic matter is not SOC, and qualitative depth classes are not demonstrated depth-to-bedrock. These nine remaining species-variable positions stay in `mahdavi_values.json`, alongside all 24 variable-area pairs, raw map labels and source scores. They were not discarded or forced into unrelated variables.

Mahdavi's mapped area is mainly labelled low forest; the text describes degraded Zagros forest. Species records do not authorise conversion of intact native grassland. Its 30-ha plantation comparison measures spatial overlap, not establishment survival. Several reported hectares and percentages disagree; some final species areas exceed component suitable areas, so an intersection of all Boolean masks cannot be assumed. Source sampling depth is 0–30 cm in the methods but 35–0 cm in Table 1. These conflicts prevent numeric calibration; they do not erase demonstrated variable use.

CEDAGRO explicitly recommends full-area planting mainly where natural-regeneration potential is low. FAO likewise identifies degraded sites without regeneration sources as candidates for planting. Their two `regeneration_potential` records carry decreasing direction **for selecting active planting relative to passive regeneration**, not for ecological survival. They do not say that more degraded sites have higher planting success. CEDAGRO's third structural record retains extreme soil acidity as a condition requiring remediation before planting; direction is unclear across the full pH range, no depth or numeric boundary is supplied, and acidity is not made a permanent exclusion.

The five operational records concern local planting-material availability and worker training in CEDAGRO, plus labour, budget and planting material in FAO. They use `operational_risk`, not structural suitability. Finance means implementation resources in this passage, not a measured credit-access score. Generic site access was not silently converted to road distance, tenure security or permission to use land.

## Credibility and remaining work

Mahdavi is a local peer-reviewed modelling study, with explicit variables and soil sampling but incomplete calibration/combination reporting and no independent establishment validation. Medium is a ceiling, not a claim of validated species limits. CEDAGRO is a recent regional expert manual with named technical authors and explicit method-selection caveats, but not an experimental assessment. FAO has strong institutional authority and named external reviewers; its qualitative site guidance is broad and partly older, with mixed secondary references. Both guidance sources receive medium tiers for these claims. No seminality bonus or independent replication is inferred from institutional status. Brazil/Iran contexts and the manual's Atlantic Forest ecology require transfer review before application elsewhere.

The manual discloses CEDAGRO/Fórum production and Diálogo Florestal support, including production-forestry interests; it is technical guidance, not independent performance validation. The FAO module names initiators, contributors and reviewers; institutional review is not experimental validation. No formal funding/COI statement was found in Mahdavi's main paper. No claim of absence of conflicts is inferred from that omission.
