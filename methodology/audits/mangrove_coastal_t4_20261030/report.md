## Three T4 assessments

- **Variable use:** this section is management guidance, not a suitability model.
  It supplies ecological requirements and method choices, with no model-use weights.
- **Direction:** relative tidal position is explicit (best above mid-tide), but neither
  a globally increasing elevation function nor a hydroperiod percentage follows.
  Excessive salinity after freshwater disruption reduces growth; sediment supply
  supports persistence under sea-level rise. Both remain raw-property mapping holds.
- **Calibration:** zero executable functions. No numeric tidal datum, hydroperiod
  conversion, salinity unit/cutoff, sediment-input scale or migration distance supplied.

Five source claims remain explicitly retained in the inventory: tidal position,
shallow-intertidal establishment, other-habitat caution, natural-regeneration method
choice, and the species-specific exposed-margin caveat (the latter has its own mapping
hold). Related EV references locate previously captured themes; they do **not** prove
these publications share a primary lineage, make the sources interchangeable or close
source coverage. No independence tally or new vote is claimed. Supported information
remains available for subsequent lineage/mapping decisions rather than being discarded
for lack of numeric calibration.

## Adversarial assessment

Performed by the coordinating agent, without independent-agent or human approval.

1. Above mid-tide does not establish time flooded below 50 percent. Tide asymmetry,
   local datums and topography preclude that conversion without supporting data.
2. A shallow-intertidal requirement differs from excluding normal tidal inundation.
   Existing water/subtidal exclusions must retain their particular source contexts.
3. The warning that planting may destroy seagrass or mudflats is not a legal mask
   and not proof that every mapped mudflat is unsuitable. Existing stronger avoidance
   guidance remains separately attributed.
4. Rhizophora exposure advice stays species/genus-specific; it cannot define the
   establishment envelope of all mangroves.
5. Sediment supply is not the sediment-retention outcome. Freshwater supply is not
   rainfall; salinity is not soil EC; subsidence is not absolute terrain elevation.
6. Repairable hydrology and pond levelling are intervention requirements, not evidence
   of an irreversible exclusion. Planting design is not landscape suitability.
7. The source explicitly distinguishes afforestation on newly created intertidal
   substrates from restoration. Historical occurrence cannot silently become the
   complete opportunity-space boundary for both practices.

All paragraphs in the restoration-principles section on PDF33 and its PDF34 summary
are accounted for. PDF24 assessment covers the determining-factor paragraphs and the
landward-space recommendation; its carbon/soil-building outcome discussion was not
extracted. No table occurs in the inspected restoration section. No claim is made that
