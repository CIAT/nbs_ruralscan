# Bera hydrology profiles for regional surface storage

On 12 November 2026 Pete approved three Bera and Mukhopadhyay (2023) profiles from India’s Kumari basin. The register and the three held mappings retain all 16 Table 3 bands and their within-criterion weights (PDF p. 5). Table 2 criterion weights (p. 4) are 0.125 for runoff, 0.124 for drainage density and 0.064 for rainfall. Shared endpoints retain the source ambiguity. A missing or out-of-range input has no shared score.

- **Runoff depth.** The study averages yearly SWAT runoff by sub-basin over 2003–2013. In the regional model a sub-basin at 650 mm falls in the 643–687 mm class (0.255). Page 9 cites high to very high runoff for gully plugs, moderate to high for check dams, and low to moderately low for farm ponds and percolation tanks. Low runoff also supports consideration of subsurface harvesting or recharge. Preserve the original studies cited for these distinctions.
- **Drainage density.** The study derives its stream network from a 30 m DEM. A density of 1.2 km/km² falls in the 1.08–1.34 class (0.252). The authors favour high density for surface storage and low density for recharge. Future use requires a compatible stream definition, delineation scale and area for measuring length per unit area.
- **Rainfall.** The rainfall layer is IMD mean annual precipitation for 2001–2020 at 0.25°. At 1,800 mm/year the source assigns the 1,766–1,884 class (0.265). The published domain spans 1,350–2,033 mm/year. Retain the moderate/high rainfall discussion and the monsoon-storage purpose.

## Rainfall and runoff overlap

The authors included both criteria in their regional model. Rainfall contributes to runoff generation. Section 3 and Figure 6 correlate factors with the final suitability map. The reported rainfall correlation of 0.12 is with that composite. The paper gives no pairwise rainfall–runoff coefficient. The rainfall map and runoff summaries also differ in time period and spatial support, and the methods do not identify the SWAT precipitation forcing as the same IMD layer. The paper offers underlying data on request (p. 16).

The accepted recipe holds both responses. Neither contributes to a shared score. For a future target area, identify the runoff model’s forcing and align rainfall and runoff to a common period and sub-basin support. Compare the paired layers and the process each criterion represents. Where both describe the same available water supply, use one representative under the project’s variable-selection method. Independent use would require evidence for distinct contributions and review of the combined weighting.
