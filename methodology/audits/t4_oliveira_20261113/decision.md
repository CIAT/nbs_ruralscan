# Oliveira et al. and regional erosion-control planning

Pete approved two evidence cards as one regional profile. Oliveira et al. (2026) selected reforestation locations to minimise modelled soil loss in Brazil’s Seridó River basin. The search covered agricultural and non-vegetated land, two of six mapped land-cover classes (PDF p. 4). Socioeconomic, legal and logistical constraints were omitted. Both classes remain available as study-domain evidence. A non-vegetated pixel in another basin therefore has no eligibility score from this study.

On PDF p. 8 the authors cite Santos et al. (2018) for difficulty establishing trees on slopes greater than 75% grade. Their erosion-control model nevertheless allocated some reforestation to steep land. Exactly 75% falls outside the stated caution; the study gives no survival score on either side. The caution retains its secondary lineage. The allocation figures belong to Oliveira’s model.

Page 8 assigns 5% of total proposed reforestation to slopes above 75%. Figure 8b on PDF p. 9 gives the escarped class 0.04% of proposed allocation. Figure 8c gives 4.84% for that class using its own area as denominator. The prose also assigns 97.16% of the allocation to flat and gently undulating terrain. Panel b shows 26.78% and 51.79% for those two classes. The reported total requires adding the 18.59% undulating class. Preserve both discrepancies. None of these allocation percentages measure planting success.

The numeric compilation policy retains both records for planning. A transferable planting rule would need compatible evidence linking land cover or slope to establishment. No shared eligibility mask, slope cutoff, weight or binding was approved.
