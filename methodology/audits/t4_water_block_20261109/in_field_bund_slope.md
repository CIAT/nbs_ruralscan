# In-field bunds and slope

Pete approved this decision on 9 November 2026. Keep the two bund siting sources separate. Keep the conservation-agriculture evidence in the register and exclude it from bund siting support and scoring. No shared slope cutoff or numeric curve is approved.

**Contour bunds for tree planting.** Critchley & Siegert (1991), FAO manual §5.3.2, recommends flat ground up to 5% grade and even terrain without gullies or rills. Runoff can overtop the bund and cause it to break at low spots. The full stated setting includes semi-arid to arid areas with 200–750 mm rainfall and soil at least 1.5 m deep, preferably 2 m. The recommendation is specific to this design.

**Bund systems in the Ronquillo watershed, Peru.** Krois & Schulte (2014), Table 4 (PDF p. 9), ranks slope in degrees. Table 5 (PDF p. 10) assigns slope a 0.39 criterion weight in that model.

| Published bund slope, degrees | Class | Study score |
|---|---|---:|
| <2–5 | Optimal | 9 |
| 5–10 | High | 7 |
| 10–18 | Moderate | 5 |
| 18–30 | Marginal | 3 |
| >30 | Unsuitable | 1 |
| No slope interval assigned | Restricted | 0 |

Retain the first band as printed. The text describes slopes below 5° as more appropriate for bunds. The table leaves the treatment of touching endpoints unclear. Exactly 30° falls in the marginal band. Above 30° the study gives 1. For comparison, a 4° field is about 7% grade, above the FAO recommendation for tree bunds but below the 5° preference discussed by Krois. The within-study weight applies only to that regional model.

**Conservation agriculture.** Sustainable Land Management in Practice (2011), PDF pp. 74–75, describes minimum tillage, mulch and crop rotation. The passage limits mechanised systems on slopes steeper than 16% and describes hand planting and animal traction on steeper land. Retain the profile for that implementation context. Its evidence ID provides an audit trail for its exclusion from the bund mapping.
