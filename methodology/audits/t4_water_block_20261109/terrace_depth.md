# Soil depth for terraces

Pete approved three source profiles on 9 November 2026. The shared terracing mapping has no numerical depth cutoff. The profiles apply to the designs and uses below.

**Krois & Schulte (2014).** In the Peruvian suitability model, Table 4 (PDF p. 9) gives this soil-depth row for terraces and bunds. Table 5 (PDF p. 10) assigns soil depth a criterion weight of 0.14 within the study model.

| Reported soil depth | Model class | Source score |
|---|---|---:|
| >1.0 m | Optimally suitable | 9 |
| 0.5–1.0 m | Highly suitable | 7 |
| 0.3–0.5 m | Moderately suitable | 5 |
| 0.2–0.3 m | Marginally suitable | 3 |
| <0.2 m | Not suitable | 1 |
| No depth range assigned | Restricted | 0 |

Exactly 1.0 m is in the high class. The printed classes share the 0.3 and 0.5 m endpoints. The table gives no tie convention there. The original class scores and criterion weight remain available for reproducing this particular model.

**Chen et al. (2017).** Table 1 (PDF p. 4) describes the application of zig terraces in fields with soil deeper than 15 cm and fairly uniform slopes, generally below 30°. Exactly 15 cm falls outside the stated depth condition. The 10–15° reverse slope in the structural-features column describes the constructed platform. The field-slope condition is the one relevant to siting.

**FAO, Sustainable Land Management in Practice (2011).** The cross-slope barriers guidance (PDF p. 113, printed p. 116) warns against bench terraces on shallow soils because of landslide risk. It supplies no numerical definition of shallow. Keep this warning specific to bench terraces.

A field with 18 cm of soil and a uniform 20° slope meets Chen's stated depth condition and typical field-slope condition for zig terraces. Krois assigns that depth the lowest scored class. FAO gives no numerical basis for classifying that site for bench terraces. Applying one depth cutoff across all three would erase those differences. The source soil measurements have no verified common depth-to-bedrock layer. The [run record](terrace_depth_run.json) pins the evidence, decision, agent settings and accepted row for replay.
