# Slope evidence for terracing

Pete approved retaining five profiles by design on 9 November 2026, in each publication's original units. Four sources inform terrace siting. The fifth describes water-harvesting stone lines and is kept outside terrace scoring. No shared maximum or pooled curve is approved.

| Source and locator | Design-specific site guidance |
|---|---|
| Krois & Schulte (2014), PDF p. 9, Table 4, terrace row | In degrees, 0–2 scores 1 (not suitable), 2–5 scores 3 (marginal), 5–10 scores 5 (moderate), 10–18 scores 7 (high), 18–30 scores 9 (optimal) and >30 scores 7 (high). Restricted (0) has no terrace slope range. Exactly 30° is optimal. Other touching bands retain the printed endpoints. Table 5, PDF p. 10, gives slope a criterion weight of 0.39 in this model. |
| Chen et al. (2017), PDF p. 4, Table 1, scope of application | Level terraces: <25° for rice, drought-tolerant crops and orchards. Zig terraces: soil >15 cm deep and fairly uniform slopes, generally <30°. Slope-separated terraces: 15–20° with permeable soil in low-rainfall areas. Slope terraces: <7° with thin soil cover. Fanya juu: thick cover and steep uneven slopes where benching is unsuitable. Half-moon terraces: steep slopes with shallow stony soil and short steep slopes for tree survival. These are field conditions. Constructed platform, ridge and trench dimensions are separate. |
| FAO, Sustainable Land Management in Practice (2011), PDF p. 113, cross-slope barriers | Fanya juu on moderate to steep slopes up to 50% grade. In dry areas this design requires rainwater harvesting. Fanya chini on moderate to hilly slopes up to 35% grade. |
| WOCAT (2013), PDF p. 129, forward-sloping benches | Up to 60% grade in the described system, with annual rainfall of 200–600 mm, mainly for trees or bushes. Runoff comes from the uncropped land between terraces. The reported catchment-to-application area ratio is 1:1–10:1. |
| Critchley & Siegert (1991), FAO manual §§5.7.1–2 | Preferably below 2% grade for contour stone lines harvesting crop water. The manual distinguishes this practice from hillside terracing. Retain the full source record as contextual evidence outside terrace calibration and support. |

## Check on a common site

At 27°, grade is approximately 51% (100 × tan of the slope angle). This site is in Krois’s optimal band, above the 50% Fanya juu guidance, and within the slope range WOCAT reports for forward-sloping benches. This example converts units only to compare the same site. The archived source tables keep degrees and percent grade as printed.

The Krois and Chen evidence records formerly encoded 30° as an absolute maximum. The corrected records retain source classes or design-specific guidance. Their IDs, source quotes, locators and reviewer states are preserved. The approved disposition and source weight remain separate from runtime scoring and weights.
