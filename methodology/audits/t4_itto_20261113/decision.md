# Native vegetation conversion excludes proposed tree planting

ITTO (2020), Guiding Element 20, PDF p. 36 (printed p. 35), directs that planted forests should not replace existing native tropical grasslands, wetlands or savannas. Pete approved a broader project criterion on 13 November 2026 covering existing indigenous natural vegetation. The guideline also cautions against converting peatlands. Habitat identity and the proposed planting determine whether conversion would occur.

Apply the exclusion to active forest planting (including enrichment where it would displace native vegetation) and establishment of production plantations. The plantation family retains its existing roll-up status. The same evidence record supports both applications. Natural regeneration is outside the scope of a planting-conversion rule.

The assessed mask records confirmed conversion as 1, confirmed absence of conversion as 0, and unknown as missing. Confirmed conversion excludes the planting footprint even at maximum biophysical suitability. Confirmed absence passes this criterion alone. Uncertain or unassessed locations stay unknown. A native savanna remains excluded if the proposed trees are themselves native. A degraded native wetland forest can pass when the planting restores the forest without replacing indigenous vegetation. An unverified grassland pixel cannot establish either outcome.
