# Cook-Patton’s definition of forest restoration and cited regrowth observations

Pete approved three cards for passive forest regeneration on 13 November 2026. On PDF p. 2, Cook-Patton et al. (2020) define restoration as a transition from less than 25% to more than 25% tree cover on historically forested land. A change from 24% to 26% there meets their definition. The wording leaves an endpoint of exactly 25% unspecified. A present-day pixel with 24% cover alone establishes neither historical forest nor a transition. The evidence register retains both bounds and their meaning.

On PDF p. 7 the authors discuss earlier observations of more frequent regrowth near rivers or existing forest fragments and on steep, less-accessible slopes. They cite references 40–43 together. Both cards share that citation group as their origin. Count the passage once and check the individual references before combining these observations with primary studies. The global carbon-rate map supplies no independent distance or slope estimate.

The numeric compilation policy keeps all three cards available for interpretation. A shared canopy, proximity or slope score would need site-targeting evidence that relates measured conditions to passive regeneration under comparable land use. Each shared cutoff remains open. No binding, interpolation, missing-value score or default weight was approved.
