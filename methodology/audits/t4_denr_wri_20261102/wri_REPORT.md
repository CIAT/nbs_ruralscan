## Findings

1. **Retain one qualitative passive-regeneration candidate.** Box 5, native page 38 / printed 34, explicitly says insufficient native source populations and impaired seed dispersal can prevent tree reemergence. `regeneration_potential` is the closest VONT match. FAM `forest_restoration__passive_regeneration` matches the explicit source system. The candidate's exact contiguous passage excludes external seed procurement. Direction is increasing availability/capacity; no numeric calibration. Do not replace it with the FMNR-specific `rootstock_presence` alias. It is authors' expert interpretation, not proof of a necessary/sufficient causal condition (pages 31–32). New candidate remains unapproved and un-ingested.
2. **Do not lose physical-factor use merely because calibration is absent.** Tables 4/7 and Box 5 explicitly assess soil, rainfall, temperature and fire individually despite one combined diagnostic response. This is qualitative diagnostic use, not use in a fitted suitability calculation. Raw soil property, climate time aggregation and fire metric are unspecified; mapping to pH, annual rainfall, mean annual temperature or wildfire probability would invent specificity. Retain these raw criteria with mapping holds. No source weights exist, so the combined row cannot donate weights to its members.
3. **Directional impediment evidence exists.** Page 38 states invasive plants and uncontrolled livestock impede recovery; pages 48–49 repeat and propose removal. Lack of a matching invasion/uncontrolled-grazing metric is an ontology hold, not absence of relationship. `grazing_system_present` would be a wrong proxy. Do not turn threat-removal implementation guidance into a generic hazard threshold.
4. **Appendix 2 is an important omitted-method risk.** Native page 75 and endnotes 93 describe actual global opportunity mapping with climate/soil, ecoregions, potential-versus-actual canopy, exclusion of open croplands and settlements, and inclusion of former-forest livestock pastures. Retain the 10% potential canopy definition and 1-km map grain as source values. Neither is a calibrated establishment threshold. Global atlas is explicitly too coarse for national planning; no single restoration family is isolated. Primary mapping-method attribution and transfer holds remain. Page 25's >500 km remoteness is operational/implementation context, not a T4 hard cutoff.
5. **Natural-ecosystem safeguard retained.** Appendix 1 says not to convert natural forests, grasslands or other ecosystems. This is restoration-baseline/do-no-harm guidance, not permission to exclude all degraded forest or to afforest every open ecosystem. Endnote 4's 25% natural/10% current savanna example is illustrative, not a universal canopy optimum.
6. **Operational factors remain referrals.** Markets, tenure, policy, finance, extension, participation, institutional coordination and implementation design belong to M2b/M6 consideration; no operational or T3/T6 units were extracted. Clearing restrictions in Table 7 are governance incentives for restoration, not statements that restoration is prohibited in protected areas.
7. **Readiness is not suitability.** Green/yellow/red means in place/partly/not in place (page 43), not high/medium/low ecological suitability. Table 5's absent dot means insufficient evidence, not absence. Rwanda's >=20% native planting target, Brazil nursery doubling, historical restored hectares and leakage percentages are not T4 cutoffs or weights.
