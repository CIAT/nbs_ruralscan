## Retained relationships and holds

- UNEP PDF21 explicitly recommends low, muddy seaward planting sites with constant salinity for *Sonneratia alba*, and dry high-salinity landward sites for *Ceriops tagal* and *Avicennia marina*. Three species-specific inventory records preserve those claims. “Muddy” is not yet a USDA texture-class crosswalk; local tidal position is not generic elevation; constant salinity is not low salinity. No whole-family rule is inferred.
- UNEP PDF59 reports Rufiji planting experience: seedlings and saplings grow better in less saline soils. That supported direction is retained as an ontology hold. No canonical salinity variable exists in the current VONT; rainfall, drainage and pH are not substitutes. No numerical calibration is supplied.
- Nursery substrate, planting depth, pots, spacing, sampling design and observation waiting times remain implementation information. UNEP Table8’s regeneration height classes are monitoring categories. Table10 describes 19 crab/mollusc observation rows, not tree planting requirements.
- FFEM Table7’s arrows indicate convergence toward reference-site conditions and disappearance of invasive species. They do not imply increasing suitability with every listed variable. RAM and the SER recovery wheels remain condition/recovery assessment methods, not spatial establishment models.
- FFEM PDF58–60 report combined restoration outcomes. Salinity changes, invasive removal, survival and habitat recovery are retained for an effect-lane review with context and comparators; no T4 thresholds are derived. Valuation and cultural-use annexes describe services, products and monitoring indicators, not measured restoration benefits or clinical advice.
