# Busch et al. (2019) and pantropical forest-cover change

Pete approved the three forest-restoration cards as one pantropical profile. Busch et al. modelled changes in forest cover across 90 countries. Their 2000–2010 observations include transitions arising from human activity and natural processes. The paper discusses observed terrain associations on PDF pp. 1–2 and defines the classification and sensitivity runs on PDF p. 5.

The base run classified forest using 30% tree cover. Using 10% and 50% instead reduced projected additional removals from reforestation at US$20/tCO₂ by 76% and 65%, respectively, relative to the base run. These values quantify sensitivity of the modelled carbon result to the forest definition. For example, a pixel with 29% cover falls below the 30% definition and above the 10% definition. Neither classification establishes whether planting would succeed there.

Reforestation was significantly greater on flatter land and at lower elevations. Retain both associations from the same historical analysis. The observations do not distinguish planting from spontaneous regrowth. Cook-Patton’s cited steep-slope observation concerns passive regrowth on less-accessible land. Averaging the two slope findings would obscure their different populations and outcomes.

All three cards remain available to planning. The compilation policy keeps shared active-planting scores open. Evidence separating planting from spontaneous regrowth and linking terrain to planting outcomes would support a future review. No shared thresholds, default weights, dataset bindings or missing-value treatment were inferred.
