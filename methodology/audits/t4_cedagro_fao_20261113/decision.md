# Choosing active planting and identifying sites needing soil treatment

Pete approved three qualitative active-planting cards on 13 November 2026. CEDAGRO’s Espírito Santo manual recommends planting across the whole site mainly in agricultural landscapes where natural regeneration potential is low and nearby forest fragments are scarce (PDF p. 13). Table 5, footnote 1 (p. 45), defines a potential native forest seed source as at least 3 ha and at least at mid-stage regeneration. The 500 m reference depends on dispersal conditions. A site substantially downslope from the source may receive propagules over a greater distance. A physical barrier may shorten that distance. Even a 4 ha fragment within 500 m needs checks on regeneration stage and dispersal barriers.

CEDAGRO lists extremely acidic soil among examples of severe physical or chemical degradation requiring treatment before planting (p. 14). Acidity flags a prerequisite intervention. The manual supplies no numerical pH boundary.

The FAO forest restoration module gives two reasons planting may be needed. A degraded site may lack natural regeneration sources, or management objectives may favour more intensive intervention. The latter is a choice of management strategy. The Spanish snapshot locates both conditions under “Seleccionar el método o los métodos de restauración”, in the paragraph beginning “En sitios degradados”.

The two sources support choosing a restoration method. Their evidence does not quantify seedling survival. CEDAGRO’s seed-source settings remain specific to its regional guidance. All three cards remain available to planning. Shared distance and pH cutoffs remain open. No weights, layer bindings or scores for missing observations were introduced.
