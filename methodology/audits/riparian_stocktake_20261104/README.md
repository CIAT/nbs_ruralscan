## What this changes

The scoping recommendation remains **zonal-linear riparian products**. First identify the watercourse, current land cover and restoration opportunity; then distinguish ecological connectivity, intercepted runoff and management priorities. Stream adjacency alone does not prove that runoff crosses the buffer, that vegetation will establish, or that a particular width is optimal.

`criteria.csv` retains 28 source-linked criteria/method groups, each with native PDF pages and a routing/transfer limit. These are an audit inventory, **not EV units or approved response curves**. Source manifest includes canonical metadata, exact team-library paths, PDF hashes and targeted pages. All 11 PDFs already exist in the shared stocktake library. Scope is selected methods, identity, limitations and key figures—not exhaustive page, supplement or citation-chain extraction.

## Corrections and holds that matter

1. **Semmens land-use direction:** the stocktake describes identifying non-cropland for milkweed restoration. Primary p. 3 explicitly restricts conversion to current agricultural land. Existing natural vegetation and long-lived orchard crops are excluded from that conversion analysis. Orchard exclusion reflects retirement practicality, not inability of riparian vegetation to establish. Fixed widths are 30 or 100 m **per side**. The variable 80/100/120 m wording is not equally explicit about side convention; do not infer it from Bentrup.
2. **Bentrup width convention:** 80/100/120 m are **total corridor widths**, with 40 m on each bank explicitly given for first-order streams. They define inventory windows, not planting prescriptions. The 0.1-ha remnant and 0.16-km gap thresholds are local coarse-filter choices, not a calibrated global T4 curve. Grassland was omitted because the map confounded native grassland with hay/non-native pasture; do not convert that data limitation into an ecological exclusion.
3. **Gu resolution:** 30-m land cover was resampled to 5 m to count land within narrow buffers. Authors explicitly say this does not improve accuracy. Retain original information grain and linear geometry; do not report a 5-m suitability product supported by 30-m classifications.
4. **Elkadiri numbers and practice scope:** p. 9 reports mean sediment yield 8.94 and SD 5.24 Mg/ha/year, while pp. 13–14 reverse their roles and use 5.25. Figure 5 visually supports approximately 3.7, 14.2 and 19.4 thresholds. Retain the contradiction; do not silently repair the paper or transfer local classes. Stream-order 1/2 selection on p. 11 applies to **sediment basins**, not buffers. No constructed-wetland expansion follows.
5. **Witing action boundary:** urban and water areas are excluded from reforestation. The 100-m total corridor is a model boundary, not a prescribed buffer width. The exploratory DeFor20% option is explicitly discussed as incompatible with current policy/legal conditions (p. 13); it must not enter our NbS action set. Cotton-strip tensile strength is an ecosystem-process proxy, not a direct carbon-sequestration measurement.
6. **Evidence role:** sediment hotspots, agricultural opportunity cost, biodiversity targets and optimal allocation belong in priority/trade-off reasoning. Roads and practical retirement constraints need operational context. Flow bypass affects service delivery. These are not all interchangeable structural-suitability variables.

The five direct buffer-placement sources are US, Canadian or Belgian applications. This fixed set does **not** establish LMIC transferability. Chinese and Indonesian context in the referrals cannot be counted as validation of the retained buffer methods. A bounded stocktake pass is complete; literature saturation is not claimed.
