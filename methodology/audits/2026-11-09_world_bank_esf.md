## What the framework requires

The Bank’s policy describes its due diligence and project risk classification (pp. 3–11). The borrower uses the applicable standards to assess effects, agree management actions and monitor them throughout the project. ESS1 sets the overall approach (pp. 15–29). Consider alternatives early and first seek to avoid harm. Where impacts persist, minimise and mitigate them, then consider compensation or offsets for significant residual impacts where feasible. ESS10 covers stakeholder identification, accessible information, meaningful engagement and grievance handling (pp. 97–100).

## Interpretation for the scan

M2b estimates exposure of a proposed investment to hazards. The ESF also asks what the proposed investment could do to people and the environment. A useful hand-off records the proposed activity, potentially affected groups or habitats, the possible impact, evidence gaps, and who should investigate these during preparation. The Bank determines the formal project risk classification.

ESS7 paragraph 24 (pp. 79–80) specifies three circumstances for free, prior and informed consent (FPIC) involving affected peoples covered by the standard. These concern adverse effects on traditionally owned or customarily used land and resources, relocation from those lands or resources, and significant effects on cultural heritage integral to the group’s identity or cultural, ceremonial or spiritual life. M2b, M3 and M6 currently describe an automatic FPIC flag from spatial overlap. Treat that discrepancy as open for safeguards review. A mapped overlap can prompt inquiry. Incomplete mapping also makes local verification necessary where no overlap appears.
