# Integrated fire management — proposed family scope

**Status: draft for methodological review, 3 November 2026.** This document restores explicit consideration of an original stocktake target. It does not register new FAM/T0 rows, approve thresholds, or authorize field burns. Evidence basis: [six-paper stocktake reconciliation](../audits/fire_management_stocktake_20261103/README.md).

## Proposed two-family structure

| Provisional family | Included intervention concept | Dominant limiting factor | Proposed spatial product | Source anchors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ecological fire-regime management | Planned ecological/prescribed fire and appropriate reintroduction of natural/cultural fire regimes in fire-adapted systems | Ecosystem compatibility with fire and locally appropriate regime; existing habitat/vegetation system must be identified first | `applicability_zone` for landscape scoping; not a burn prescription or schedule | Nocentini pp. 6–14; Rodrigues pp. 3–4; Nepal p. 9 |
| Ecologically compatible vegetation/fuel management | Management of existing vegetation/fuel structure or landscape configuration to reduce damaging-fire spread while retaining/restoring ecosystem functions | Existing treatable vegetation/fuel system and ecological acceptability of the specified treatment | `applicability_zone`; particular linear components must retain their geometry and cannot inflate treated hectares | Rodríguez-Fernández pp. 7–9; Rodrigues pp. 3–7 |

These families are separated by their dominant ecological constraint, not by their shared exposure to wildfire. The second family is **not blanket endorsement of biomass removal, clearing, plantation intensification or all firebreak construction as NbS**. A candidate project must specify the ecological intervention and outcomes. These papers support management planning, not every possible treatment variant. Grazing-based fuel management and culturally specific Indigenous practices were not adequately characterised in this six-paper set; do not invent subfamilies or claim they were assessed.

The same landscape can support both families, but resulting areas must be deduplicated when reporting a combined opportunity footprint. Time since last fire and biomass/fuel condition help prioritise within an ecologically appropriate domain; they must not substitute for that domain. Some threats to fire-sensitive vegetation will instead route to forest protection or wetland restoration, preserving the separate intervention identity.

## Scoping workflow

1. Identify existing ecosystem/vegetation and intended ecological management objective. Unknown fire compatibility remains an explicit unresolved context, not a default “suitable” mask.
2. Identify compatible intervention family and management units. Retain local habitat/reference-regime information and restoration/protection overlaps.
3. Characterise need and potential benefit using fire history, fuel structure and hazard/transmission information, without treating modeled hazard as treatment suitability.
4. Keep access, suppression/control capability, smoke/receptors and implementation constraints in the project-risk/hand-off stream. Local expert planning remains required; this toolkit is not an operational burn-plan generator.
5. Rank scenarios transparently against TTL priorities and retain uncertainty. Country/biome context must govern any response parameters; US/European priority scales are examples, not global defaults.

## Explicit exclusions and interfaces

- Detection towers, monitoring networks, fire stations, vehicles and hard suppression infrastructure: supporting implementation services, not a landscape NbS family. Wang 2025 remains an M6 referral.
- Generic wildfire susceptibility/risk maps: M2/M4 context. Sakti 2022 remains relevant without becoming a prescribed-fire eligibility model.
- Natural/cultural fire reintroduction: a source-supported concept conditional on local ecological and social appropriateness; no cultural-practice validation or consent assumed from the cited models.
- Mangroves: not an extension of ecological burning merely because they have a separate NbS recipe. Nocentini explicitly excludes them from its burn targets.
- Rooftop harvesting and homegardens are outside the user's target scope and do not enter this fire-management family work.

## What would be needed to activate this scope

A single reviewed family/recipe decision must establish the NbS identity and these family definitions, then update the manifest-controlled registers through the normal schema workflow. Evidence units would need source/locator verification, family-specific roles and no automatic human approval. Dataset bindings must distinguish observed ecosystem/fire histories from modeled hazard and scenario assumptions. No existing stocktake priority weights should be hardcoded.

Current bounded evidence supports proposing these families, **not declaring either globally calibrated or LMIC-validated**. No new discovery is automatically commissioned by this draft.
