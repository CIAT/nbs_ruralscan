How to read Figure 3.1

  1. Four classes, one rule set. Very high to Low ranks how well each cell fits the practice’s envelope. Select classes in the control bar: ticked, filled buttons are included; empty buttons are not. Only selected classes stay coloured on the map. Every summary and later stage uses this extent.
  2. Hatched with an outline is excluded; dotted is no data. Excluded cells are removed by law or land cover (towns, mangrove and wetland, protected areas) — not low suitability. Dotted cells lack an input (here, a cloud gap) and are counted as neither.
  3. A cell is not a farm. Each 5 km cell is 2,500 ha. A suitable cell says the envelope fits; it does not say every field in it will take trees.
  4. 2050 moves the envelope, not the farms. The same rules on mid-century climate show where conditions drift in or out of fit. It is not a forecast of what farmers will plant.

3.1.2Excluded, low and no data are differentOnly one of them is a judgement about the land.

Excluded means the practice may not go there whatever the conditions. Low means it could go there but the envelope fits poorly. No data means an input is missing, so the cell is neither counted in nor ruled out. Merging any two of these would misstate the area.

3.1.3Roads, markets and tenure are not on this mapThey are levers a project can change, so they appear in Stage 04.

Distance to roads, market access, extension services and land tenure shape whether a practice is taken up, but investment can change them. Baking them into suitability would hide the places a project could open up. They are shown as a separate operational screen alongside project risk.
