Which practices deserve closer investigation?

Set a budget and read the three practices side by side: how much room each has, how well it meets the need, what the evidence says it does, and roughly what it costs. You take away a shortlist to carry into feasibility — not a ranking.

Areas are nominal: cells × 2,500 ha. Riparian buffers are reported as kilometres of stream zone and are not comparable with hectares. Costs are ranges from mixed sources, undiscounted; affordable area = budget ÷ cost, highest to lowest cost.

How to read this

  1. Footprint is room, not a target. It is where the practice could go, before anyone asks a farmer.
  2. Overlap is not compatibility. Two practices can share a cell on the map and still compete for the same field.
  3. Strength and confidence are separate. A strong benefit seen in few studies is still low confidence.

What the budget shows

  1. Affordable area is an order of magnitude. It tells you whether the budget buys a pilot or a programme, not what a project would cost.
