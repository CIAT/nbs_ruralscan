Methodology notes

How NbS inclusion is reviewed

Supporting notes on practice membership, ecological fit and evidence review. These procedures explain how profiles are assessed; they are not steps a Task Team Leader must complete to explore practices.

Back to 2.1 · What it is

When does a practice count as a nature-based solution?

An NbS addresses a human need through ecosystem care and natural processes, with benefits for biodiversity as well as people. Natural and modified ecosystems can both contribute. See the UNEP overview. A technology name or a positive yield result alone does not establish that connection.

Engineered components can support an ecological approach. Their presence alone does not settle inclusion; the World Bank explainer describes combined approaches and cautions that planting is not automatically beneficial for biodiversity.

  1. Does it belong to this practice and family? Check the intervention’s purpose, components and design. A shared benefit does not make two practices interchangeable.
  2. Does it function as an NbS here? Name the ecosystem action, the natural process delivering a service, the people who benefit, and a credible biodiversity or ecosystem-integrity pathway. Distinguish reported evidence from assumptions.
  3. Does the evidence answer this question? Check the outcome, comparison, context and attribution. Passing the first two checks does not make a site description a suitability threshold or make a livelihood benefit proof of infrastructure protection.
What context must the desk review consider?

Record the starting habitat and what would change; effects on connected land and water systems; rights, participation and distribution of benefits and burdens; and how management could last. These questions draw on the IUCN criteria, including ecological outcomes, equity and adaptive management.

Uncertainty is visible. This scoping workflow requires a credible ecological pathway, not already-measured biodiversity net gain in every source. Missing core support or a material unresolved conflict means a hold. Do not erase conflicting findings with generic conditions. Carry remaining implementation questions into feasibility, design and implementation.

Apply the same checks across all six project practices

The prompts below apply the project’s inclusion workflow. They are questions for review, not acceptance decisions or a technology whitelist. The three selectable profiles in Section 2 remain worked examples.

Practice-specific questions within the shared inclusion checks
PracticeQuestion to make explicit
AgroforestryWhat ecological function is expected from the tree–farming combination, and what biodiversity pathway accompanies the production benefit?
Forest restorationWhat ecosystem is being recovered, compared with its starting condition? Increased tree cover alone cannot answer that question.
Mangrove restorationWhat coastal ecosystem process would recover, and what evidence supports the proposed site and intervention?
Riparian buffersWhat service would the vegetated strip deliver, and what changes could occur beyond it? Floodplain reconnection remains a separate project family.
Wetland managementWhat water and ecosystem functions would the intervention sustain, and how would connected areas and users be affected?
Water harvesting and conservationProject scope rule: strongly demonstrated water harvesting can qualify even as a secondary function; weak or incidental secondary benefits cannot. The source must support capture, retention or soil-water storage and an intended use or beneficiary pathway in the actual component. A tank or measured effect size is not required in every case. A generic infiltration claim or technology label is insufficient. Ambiguous evidence stays held; NbS fit, biodiversity or ecosystem pathways and safeguards remain separate checks.
What do the review outcomes mean?
Included for scoping
Recorded practice and ecological checks pass without unresolved conditions. Claim-level evidence review still applies.
Conditional
The core pathway is supported; explicit limits or implementation conditions must accompany its use.
Held
Purpose, core pathway or a material conflict remains unresolved, or required definition access is missing. Keep it out of accepted outputs while clarification is pending.
Excluded
Evidence establishes a practice mismatch or failure of NbS criteria. Record which reason applies and preserve the evidence; do not silently relabel or delete it.

These are project scoping outcomes, not certification or site-feasibility findings. Existing approvals require reassessment before current reuse; no status is automatically assigned to the worked examples in Section 2.

Illustrative boundary example: water harvesting

An erosion-control intervention may also qualify as water harvesting when sources strongly demonstrate that its actual component captures or retains water for an intended use, including soil-water storage. A passing mention of improved infiltration does not establish that pathway. Ambiguous cases stay held. Ecological fit and safeguards still need separate review. These are examples of the project rule, not decisions about any specific source or technology.
