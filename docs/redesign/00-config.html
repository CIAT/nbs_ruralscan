Do you need this? Only if an exclusion does not fit the country or the practice.

C.4Correlation and clustering

Do you need this? Only to see why some variables are not used directly.

Download PNG

Illustrative within-theme grouping: variables linked at or above this threshold form a cluster. Choose one representative per cluster. These synthetic correlations do not rerun the analytical pipeline.

Figure C.2 · Example correlation matrix and clusters for six variables in the area of interest; the representative of each cluster is underlined

Why one per cluster. Variables that move together carry the same signal. Using all of them would count that signal several times and quietly raise its weight.

How it works. Variables are first grouped by theme in the recipe, then clustered by correlation within the area of interest. One representative per cluster enters the score; the other members stay listed beside it.

Selection rationale. Defaults use precipitation and temperature as familiar source variables, with slope separate. The selected representatives above and underlined matrix labels update together. A different choice changes the local preview selection only.

C.5Opportunity-space variable classes

How do low and high values become map classes?

Choose a variable, check whether higher values mean better or worse conditions, then split its distribution into 2, 3 or 5 classes. People and land indicators use Better/Worse labels; climate hazards use Low/Moderate/High risk (plus Very low/Very high with five classes). These describe relative conditions under the cut points, not loss probabilities. Quantiles divide baseline example cells into roughly equal groups (three groups are terciles); thresholds use explicit cut points. High poverty means a higher poverty indicator; high drought means a higher drought-hazard indicator, not a combined risk score.

Selected variable only: 3 quantile classes; higher values mean worse. Cut points recalculate from the baseline opportunity extent.

Synthetic indicator values rescaled to 0–100 over eligible example cells; not poverty percentages or hazard probabilities. Preview is restricted to the current opportunity-space selection. Quantile breaks use baseline values within the baseline opportunity extent for the selected classes; the same breaks classify baseline and future values. Future indicator values may exceed 100. Valid edits are saved per variable and applied to the maps and distributions in Section 3.2 · People and land and Section 3.3 · Climate hazards. Scientific recipes and evidence remain unchanged.
