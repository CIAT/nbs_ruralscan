Stage 02 · Opportunity space

Where could planted silvoarable agroforestry establish?

Read the map of where the practice’s climate, terrain, soils and farming system fit, with legal exclusions removed. You take away the opportunity space — the Very high and High cells that every later stage works inside.

Boundaries: geoBoundaries gbOpen v6 (CC-BY 4.0); neighbouring coastlines approximate. Cells: synthetic example surface (seed 20261031) — no thresholds, weights or evidence.

How to read this

  1. Four classes, one rule set. Very high to Low ranks how well each cell fits the practice’s envelope. Only Very high and High go forward.
  2. Hatched is excluded, dotted is unknown. Hatched cells are removed by law or land cover (towns, mangrove and wetland, protected areas) — not low suitability. Dotted cells lack data (here, a cloud gap) and are not counted as either.
  3. A cell is not a farm. Each 5 km cell is 2,500 ha. A suitable cell says the envelope fits; it does not say every field in it will take trees.
  4. 2050 moves the envelope, not the farms. The same rules on mid-century climate show where conditions drift in or out of fit. It is not a forecast of what farmers will plant.

Excluded, low and unknown are differentOnly one of them is a judgement about the land.

Excluded means the practice may not go there whatever the conditions. Low means it could go there but the envelope fits poorly. Unknown means an input is missing, so the cell is neither counted in nor ruled out. Merging any two of these would misstate the area.

Roads, markets and tenure are not on this mapThey are levers a project can change, so they appear in Stage 03.

Distance to roads, market access, extension services and land tenure shape whether a practice is taken up, but investment can change them. Baking them into suitability would hide the places a project could open up. They are shown as a separate operational screen alongside project risk.

Next, test these places against hazards to the investment. Fire and flood can damage young trees before they pay back.

Stage 03 · Project risk

Which of these places would put the planting itself at risk?

The same opportunity space, screened for fire and flood during the years the trees take to establish. You take away a flag on each place — a hazard to the investment is a filter, never added to suitability or need.

Boundaries: geoBoundaries gbOpen v6 (CC-BY 4.0). Hazard cells: synthetic example surface (seed 20261031) — not a hazard model.

How to read this

  1. Only the opportunity space is coloured. Everything else is left as paper, because a hazard screen only matters where the practice could go.
  2. This is risk to the planting, not to people. Risk to rural livelihoods is a need layer and belongs with the hotspots in Stage 04.
  3. A flag, not a subtraction. High exposure does not lower suitability. It asks for design changes — firebreaks, species choice, timing — or a different site.
