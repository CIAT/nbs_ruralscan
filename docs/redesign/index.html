A method for scoping where nature-based solutions could work in rural landscapes

What the project is, what this visual wireframe is for, and how to read the seven stages that follow. Everything shown is example data on real Sierra Leone boundaries.

H.1What this project is

The Rural NbS Scan is a World Bank-funded methodology (project D591, led by the Alliance of Bioversity International and CIAT) for the spatial prioritisation of nature-based solutions in rural, agricultural and forestry landscapes. It helps World Bank task team leaders scope where different practices could be invested in, what their footprint would be, and which project priorities — poverty, biodiversity, climate risk, gender equity — those footprints meet.

The project delivers three things:

  • A methodology toolkit — a cross-cutting framework and a recipe for each practice.
  • A pilot implementation report — the method applied in two least-developed-country pilots.
  • Reproducible Jupyter and Colab notebooks — the contracted delivery format for the pilot analyses.

Scoping, not feasibility. The scan does no site design, no full cost–benefit analysis and no ecosystem-service modelling. Those come later, and the scan points to them in its final stage.

H.2What this wireframe is for

These pages are a visual explanation of the method and a sketch of the interface a task team could use. Each stage shows its question, its inputs, the evidence behind it and what it hands on, so that the project team and the Bank can agree the method before it is built into notebooks.

It is a demonstrator: not the final interface and not a contracted product. Everything on these pages is example data on real Sierra Leone boundaries; no figure is a pilot result.

Who it is for

World Bank task team leaders and their country counterparts scoping rural NbS investments before any feasibility study — and the project team and reviewers who shape the method.

H.3How the method flows

Each stage answers one question and hands its result to the next.

  1. 01Set the scopeWhat problem, which practices, where?
  2. 02Explore the practicesWhich practices could answer this?
  3. 03Map the opportunity spaceWhere could the practice establish?
  4. 04Screen project riskWould hazards put the planting at risk?
  5. 05Find priority hotspotsWhere does it meet the project’s priorities?
  6. 06Compare practicesWhich deserve a closer look?
  7. 07Plan next stepsWhat must feasibility confirm?

A user leaves with four scoping-grade products:

  • An opportunity mapwhere each practice could establish
  • A hotspot shortlistwhere that overlaps the project’s priorities
  • A practice comparisonfootprint, evidence and indicative cost
  • A draft scoping briefwhat to check next, and with whom

H.4What it is and is not

What this is

Scoping
A first, country-scale view of where to look, before anyone visits a site.
A method demonstrator
Shows how the stages fit together so the team can agree the method.
Example data
Maps use an authored surface that behaves like real inputs.

What it is not

Feasibility
No site design, cost–benefit analysis or ecosystem-service model. Those follow.
The final interface
The contracted delivery is a set of reproducible notebooks.
A pilot result
No figure on these pages describes Sierra Leone.

Four things stay separate throughout: whether a practice can establish (suitability), who needs it (livelihood need), what the evidence says it does (NbS response), and what could damage the investment (project risk). Project risk is a flag, never added to the others.

H.5How to use these pages

Control bar
Under the stage bar on every stage: practice, suitability classes, view and climate. Changes carry across pages.
Sub-tabs
Each stage opens on a simple view; numbered sub-tabs under the main figure hold the detail.
Technical configuration
Datasets, suitability functions, exclusions, clustering, climate framing, and saving or loading a project. The walkthrough never needs it.
Review mode
Switch on “Review” in the version bar to comment on any figure, table or section, highlight text or take a screengrab. Notes stay in your browser; export them as a report or open a prefilled GitHub issue.
Version selector
Switch between v0 (original mock-up), v1 (draft) and v2 (this version) in the version bar.
History
What changed between versions, and every World Bank and team request with what was done about it.
