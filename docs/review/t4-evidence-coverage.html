MethodsX 10 (2023) 102052 The section Method validation provides an overview of the source and processing of the selected criteria (geodataset factors) used in this methodology. All data sets are georeferenced and are either vector (shapeﬁle) or raster ﬁles so that the overall processing of the geodatabases follows the speciﬁc executions of each GIS package. Therefore, the methodology description will emphasize the general procedures and tools for the creation, conversion, and analysis of each input database (EF and AF) and the combination of the information sets for the corresponding outputs (V, ES, SIPA-AFS). Step 1: natural vulnerability (V) The determination of V is the ﬁrst step in the development of this method and is based on the evaluation framework of the Land vulnerability (Vu) methodology proposed by Pissarra [16] . With this, we establish a general basis that reinforces the priority of the areas of highest risk of natural land loss in the agroecosystem, regardless of which decision-making approach may be employed. The method of identiﬁes the fragility of the natural environment in watersheds based on multiple environmental factors that characterize erosion risks, which is a measure of the exposure of land to the environment for some risk (natural soil loss). It is a multi-criteria spatial analysis implemented in GIS software, where land vulnerability (secondary data) is derived from the spatial distribution of three environmental factors: geology (G), soil (S), slope (SL), and, an anthropogenic factor: land cover - primary data. Each factor represents an agent of vulnerability and corresponds to the input of a layer (raster) in the spatial modeling of map algebra. The factors are expressed in numerical (SL) and categorical (G, S) scales, but independently are evaluated within the landscape and subsequently converted to a shared dimensionless scale (rank and weight) to allow consistent aggregation and mutual comparison within the mathematical framework of multicriteria decision analysis and thus estimate the natural vulnerability of the landscape. The MDCA method adopted was the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP), which equates and weights each criterion (factor) by the arithmetic mean of the information plan and its relative importance among the criteria calculated by the pairwise comparison matrix as proposed by Saaty [58–60] , which evaluates the relative importance in a set of weights from 1 to 9. The participatory decision-making process consulted researchers specialized in forest conservation and sustainable production. The authors technical background and their articulation with diﬀerent stakeholders - small land owners and governance agents, were also considered [9] . The factors are ranked as to relevance in the landscape structure and as to the classiﬁcation of the attributes of each factor: geology (lithological types), soil (soil types), slope (slope classes), and land cover (land use and land cover classes). Additionally, all factors are ranked by their overall inﬂuence on natural vulnerability, as a dimensionless parameter to become comparable. The weight value is the importance of each factor on the landscape scale, higher weights point to the most important parameters, and higher rankings also indicate the most vulnerable classes.
