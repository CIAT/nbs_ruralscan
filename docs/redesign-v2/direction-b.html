Why this reads as a product dashboard

  1. The rail turns the method into a menu. Seven stages look like seven equal destinations, so the order — and the hand-off from one to the next — disappears.
  2. The map fills the screen but says nothing by itself. There is no question, no title and no reading beside it; the user has to know what to look at.
  3. The inspector puts configuration on every screen. Datasets, rules and weights sit one click from a first-time reader, which invites tuning before understanding.
  4. Reading is pushed to a strip. Notes and figures become tiles at the bottom edge, competing with export buttons.

It suits a returning analyst running many scans. For a task team leader seeing the method once, direction A’s chapter pages carry the argument. Back to the overview.
